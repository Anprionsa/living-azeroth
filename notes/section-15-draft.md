## 15. Class orders

Every class in this world belongs to an order, and the world has never shown most of them. A paladin of Stormwind is a Knight of the Silver Hand by the chronicles' own account, and a Horde rogue answers to the Shattered Hand in the Cleft of Shadow or to the Deathstalkers beneath the Undercity. Their trainers keep to the capitals, and their quests send them across the whole of the map, but at no point does a character arrive anywhere that belongs to the class.

Legion gave each class a hall and seated both factions in it, on the argument that the war against the Legion had made the old allegiances too small. This document keeps the halls and declines the argument. In the first year after the Third War a Forsaken paladin and a Stormwind paladin do not share a roof, and each faction's mages are its arcane strength and are kept accordingly. Where the lore already puts the factions together, the order is shared. It does so at Moonglade, where the Cenarion Circle has always received druids of both, and at Ravenholdt, which answers to no crown. Everywhere else the orders are split.

The method is the one this document uses throughout. Forever has already built one such hold, at Bandarion Keep in the Whispering Wood, where the Forsaken paladins of Tyr's Watch are sent to learn a Light their own people distrust. The other fifteen can be found in places the world already has: a hunters' lodge on the shore of Loch Modan, an abbey that empties once its novices are grown, and a hilltop camp in Arathi where the last soldiers of Stromgarde stand guard and have nothing to give.

<span>Section 15.1 and 15.2</span>

### Strongholds, chapters and outposts

Each order keeps three kinds of ground, and each faction keeps its own of each. The stronghold stands in the world. It is a friendly settlement that already exists, raised to hold the order. It keeps the trainer, the campaign and the entry to any absorbed tree the order teaches, along with the provisions and intelligence described in 15.4. The chapters are the rooms the class already uses in its faction's capitals, and they keep the everyday services, so that no one who never travels is worse served than today. The outposts are the places the class quests already send their students, from a lone mentor at a campfire to a shrine on a mountaintop, spread across the zones each faction actually levels in.

The warriors show the shape most plainly. Refuge Pointe and Hammerfall face each other across the Arathi Highlands, with the battleground between them, and each is a small camp that the order turns into a muster. The Alliance warrior spends ordinary evenings in the Pig and Whistle in Old Town and rides to Arathi when the campaign calls for it. Stromgarde Keep, held today by the Syndicate and the Boulderfist, is what the campaign sets out to win back. Clearing it changes quest ground players already know, so it is the one revisionary step in the warrior's order and ships last.

Some holds must be raised from almost nothing. The Horde's shamans have no seat in Kalimdor, although the Horde Skyborne come into the Horde through the Earthen Ring. Freewind Post becomes that seat, and the four shrines the totem quests already visit become its outposts. The Alliance's shamans are dwarves, and the older sources make the Wildhammer the shamans among them, so the Ironforge shaman is sent at last to Aerie Peak. Forever's own dwarf shaman path already runs past Grim Batol in the Wetlands on the way.

By risk the stronghold system is Tier 2: new NPCs, rooms and quests placed in settlements that exist, invalidating nothing. By cost it sits with Section 12, because sixteen settlements need building work and most of them need writing as well.

<span>Section 15.3</span>

### The campaign and the arrival

Vanilla's class quests already come at levels 10, 20, 30, 40, 52 and 60. They read as errands for a trainer because nothing gathers them. The campaign gives them one voice and one place to return to, and it brings the character to the stronghold as an arrival rather than a discovery.

Every stronghold has an arrival level. A Forsaken priest reaches Deathknell at the start of the game. An Alliance paladin reaches Chillwind Camp in the Western Plaguelands only in the fifties. Until arrival the campaign runs through the chapters and outposts. The arrival itself is a quest: a summons, a road, and a flight master who was not there before.

Alts are the complaint Legion's halls never answered. Once one character of a class and faction has finished the campaign, later ones keep the travel it opened and may pass over the story. Nothing that raises power carries across, in keeping with the line the Legacy system already has trouble holding.

<span>Section 15.4</span>

### What a stronghold knows and keeps

A hall visited once per quest chain is empty the rest of the time. A stronghold is worth returning to when it knows something and sells something.

What it knows follows the rules of 8.11: a region and the age of the report, never a coordinate. The lodge has word of a rare beast seen in the hills. The rogues know which strongbox is worth the trouble this month. The report goes stale, so the player comes back to ask again, and the answer sends them out into the world rather than keeping them in the hall.

What it keeps is the class's own provisions, in better forms than the capital sells: larger stacks, lighter ammunition, a quiver earned rather than bought. Better here means more convenient and never more powerful. The capital's vendors stay exactly as they are, so a hub is a place a player chooses to restock and never the only place a class can function. Any repeatable work the hub offers is optional, and it pays in cosmetics, never in power.

<span>Section 15.5</span>

### The hunters, and two ways to keep a lodge

The Farstriders built their lodges far from Quel'Thalas, and three of them still stand in the Alliance's lands. Farstrider Lodge is on the shore of Loch Modan, Quel'Danil is in the Hinterlands, and Quel'Lithien is on the border of the Plaguelands, where the rangers hold against the Scourge within sight of home. Starfall Village in Winterspring, built on the same plan, becomes the northern lodge, and the Wintersaber Trainers on the rock above it keep the mounts the order rides. The Alliance hunter's order is a chain of fixed houses, one for each stage of a hunter's life.

The tauren keep the hunt differently, and their quest text says so. "For hunting is a Tauren's greatest honor," Skorn Whitecloud tells the young at Bloodhoof, and Melor Stonehoof on the Hunter Rise already sends them out after named prey from the Barrens to Dustwallow. The Horde hunter's stronghold is Camp Mojache in Feralas, raised from tents to a longhouse. The hunt itself goes out as a camp that moves. It keeps to a short list of sites fixed in advance, stays a season, and chooses its next ground by weight rather than in turn, on the same rules as the wandering trainers of 8.10. Hunter Rise and Camp Mojache can say where it went and how long ago, and nothing a hunter needs is kept only there.

The roving camp is Tier 2 by risk, because it is server state that moves, and it needs a public test realm for the datamining 8.11 already names. Without 8.10 it falls back to a fixed camp, which is Tier 1 and still worth the building.

<span>Section 15.6</span>

### Growth without a realm

The halls should grow as their members do their work: the wing restored, the forge relit, the names of the first to finish the order's long tasks set on the wall. Forever has no realm to grow within. Growth by layer breaks as layers merge and split. Growth by character alone is private, and it loses the one thing growth is for, which is a place that shows what others have done.

So a stronghold grows across the whole region, as a war effort would. Each stage opens when the region's members of the class have done enough, it opens once, and it never closes, on the model of the Lakeshire bridge in 6.9. The personal scenes of the campaign are phased per character. The wall shows a character's own firsts and those of their guild, rather than a list the size of a region.

This is the architectural piece of the section. By risk it is Tier 3, because it is world state held above the level of any one server. It should ship after the strongholds themselves, which stand without it.

<span>Section 15.7 and 15.8</span>

### Doors that close

Most strongholds are towns that every class uses. Refuge Pointe serves the whole of the Alliance in Arathi, and Tarren Mill the whole of the Horde in Hillsbrad. So the order keeps an inner room: a barracks, a crypt, a tower floor. That room is closed to other classes, as the Hall of Shadows once was to anyone who was not a rogue, and it is a sanctuary even where the town around it is contested ground. At the two shared strongholds, attacking a member of the other faction costs standing with the host. That is the remedy the Forever beta's Moonglade reports already ask for.

A door can also close on a member. Three of the absorbed trees turn a character against what the order stands for. A paladin who falls into the Blackguard finds Chillwind Camp's inner room shut and the Scholomance open. A Forsaken priest who recants into Radiance leaves Deathknell for Light's Hope. A mage who takes up Necromancy is condemned in Dalaran and ignored at the Sepulcher. In every case the chapter stays open and the trainer keeps teaching, because the order's judgment is a matter of story and never of service.

<span>Section 15.9</span>

### Where the factions meet

The orders are split, but the world keeps a few fires both sides may sit at, and each of them is already in the game.

The Kirin Tor are the plainest case. Forever has restored Dalaran in the Alterac Mountains as a living city, with its guards, its bankers and its archmagi. It is neutral, and it studies and watches the mages of both factions without belonging to either. It is an outpost of both mage orders and the stronghold of neither. It passes its students on: the Alliance Skyborne who arrive at its gates are sent to the Highlord in Stormwind, and from there to the Alliance's own order at the Tower of Azora. Across the hills the Forsaken keep their arcane watch at Tarren Mill.

The hunters have Nesingwary's camp, and a tauren at Shadowprey Village who remembers hunting beside Hemet: "Even the enmity between our peoples was forgotten." The warriors have the arenas at Gurubashi and in Dire Maul, where they meet as rivals. The druids have Moonglade, and in Forever the Cenarion camp on Mount Hyjal, where night elves and tauren already stand together.

<x-import Note label="The cost note">Sixteen strongholds is the largest art commitment in this document after new zones. None depends on another, so they ship one at a time in order of need. Moonglade and Bandarion Keep come first, because they are already built. Next come the settlements that need only building work (Refuge Pointe, Hammerfall, Chillwind Camp, Farstrider Lodge, Camp Mojache). Then come those that need quests as well.</x-import>
