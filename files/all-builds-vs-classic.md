**August 2026**

*320 reworked damage builds against the averaged Classic ladder.*

---

## 1. The reference

Mean of the two Classic Naxxramas controls, since neither is authoritative and they disagree by 4.4 points: Warcraft Tavern's medians and wowtbc.gg's top 10% parses.

Expressed as share of the top spec, which is Fury warrior in both.

**Five of nine classes have a hybrid or off-spec as their best build.** Warlock's is a 26/25 split, Priest's and Paladin's too, and Hunter's is 30/21.

That is the rework's central claim landing: **in vanilla the answer is always the 31-point build, and here it is five times out of nine.**

## 4. The ladder is preserved and the top half is inflated

Reading the middle column against the first:

**The order is intact.** Warrior, Rogue, Mage, Warlock, Hunter, Druid, Shaman, Priest, Paladin, in exactly the logged sequence.

**But five classes exceed their logged best and four fall short**, and the five that exceed are the top five. Rogue is 10.6 points up, Warlock 18.2, Mage 8.9.

**That is not band neutrality. The top half has been lifted and the bottom half has not.** Total raid output has gone up rather than staying flat, which is the thing band neutrality was supposed to prevent.

## 5. The four that fall short are the important number

Druid at 58.9% against 60.5%. Shaman at 50.1% against 51.8%. Paladin at 37.6% against 39.1%.

**Not one of the bottom four classes can reach its own logged output with any of the reworked builds available to it.** Paladin's sixteen builds span 37.1% to 37.6%, a range of half a point.

**So the rework has given the top half both more power and more choices, and given the bottom half neither.** Paladin has sixteen builds that are all the same build.

## 6. Reading the whole distribution

**Rogue's median build is 84.3%, which is its logged best.** Half of everything a rogue can build matches the best a logged rogue achieves.

**Druid's median is 40.1% against a logged 60.5%.** Half of what a druid can build is twenty points below what druids actually do.

That gap between medians is the clearest single measure of the problem: **the classes that were strong got a wide band of good options, and the classes that were weak got a narrow band of poor ones.**

## 7. What this says about the next decision

The open question was what the bottom of the ladder should be. This reframes it.

**The bottom of the ladder has not moved and the top has.** Fixing that by raising the bottom would widen total output further. Fixing it by lowering the top is the band-neutral answer and has not been attempted.

**The depth coefficient reaches both.** One number per tree, no talent edits, and it can pull Rogue, Warlock and Mage down while pushing Druid, Shaman and Paladin up until total raid output matches the logged total.

**That is a single tuning pass and it is the one the protocol described.** It has not been run because the target was never set.
